* What this prototypes

The /prism mode/ reads the system through a few units (N = 1–5) instead of aggregating the whole cohort (master doc §9.1, roadmap point 10). This note prototypes the *actor* side: each biography is read as a test particle whose trajectory reveals the state of the field. The reading is abductive and heuristic — a single actor under-determines the field — but a well-chosen handful should reproduce, in miniature, the field reading obtained from the full cohort. If they do, the probe logic is validated; this is the falsification discipline of §9.3 applied to the empirical run.

* Method

From the cohort contract table (=aid, year, structure, category, sequence=) we select trajectories that cross several structures, and for each we read three field-traces:

- *Channels proven open* — the structure→structure transitions the actor actually traverses. A crossing that succeeds shows that channel was permeable in that period; a biography that gets stuck would mark a sclerosed target.
- *Vertical fate* — the status path across the four categories (Established = 3 > Aspirant = 2 > Conservative = 1 > Disappointed = 0), with its net change and reversals. This is the actor's own reciprocity, read on the scale of one life.
- *The era's current* — whether the actor moves with the dominant tendency of their period (19th c.: the economy C draws Culture A and Media D into its orbit; 20th c.: Politics B rises and satellises art and media).

The procedure is implemented in =prism_actors.py= and runs equally on the simulator's actor-transition export, so the same reading can later be produced live from the running model.

* The five probes (an arc across the two regimes)

| Probe | Span | Path (dominant) | Vertical fate | Field reading |
|-------+------+-----------------+---------------+---------------|
| James Watson | 1818–1842 | A↔C (A) | established → disappointed (−3) | A pulled into C — radical print under repression |
| John Bedford Leno | 1849–1889 | C→A→D (A) | disappointed → aspirant (+2) | the Chartist poet routed through printing and the press |
| William Farish | 1849–1889 | A→C…D (C) | established → disappointed (−3) | the clearest single-probe case of A satellised by C |
| Joseph Burgess | 1884–1920 | D…B (D) | established → conservative (−2) | Media (D) used as a ramp toward Politics (B) |
| Tom Mann | 1875–1923 | C…B (B) | disappointed → established (+3) | carried into Politics (B) — the rise of B from the economic base |

* Result: the probes reproduce the aggregate field reading

The two early, culture-anchored probes (Watson, Farish) both /fall/ — they begin established in Culture and are pulled down into the Economy, ending disappointed (net −3 each). The two late probes rise toward Politics: Tom Mann, born into the colliery, ends established in B (net +3); Joseph Burgess uses journalism (D) as his ramp into B. A single life (N = 1) thus re-exhibits, in miniature, what the full cohort showed: in the nineteenth century the Economy draws Culture and Media into its orbit; in the twentieth, Politics rises and recruits them. The probe logic passes its check.

* Next step — the live tracer in the simulator

In the running model, the prism mode would let the user select 1–5 actors and, for each year the actor is alive, read the local field state they traverse: the permeability of the channel they attempt, whether the structure they sit in is being satellised, its regime, and the actor's own category. The actor's path would be highlighted on the scene (a brightened particle), and a side panel would report these field-traces over time. The dual /instance/ prism (a perturbed mediation instance whose ripple reveals the coupling) is the second half, to be built from an instance layer harvested from the autobiographies.
