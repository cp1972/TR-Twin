* What this protocol is for

Many qualitative master's projects rest on a small number of interviews (typically
5-7) with people who staff *mediation* or *control* instances — experts, gatekeepers,
intermediaries. This protocol shows how to turn such a study into a *fictitious but
defensible* scenario in the digital twin: a way to state a mechanistic hypothesis
("if this instance works in /this/ way, then for /this/ population the circulation
reconfigures in /that/ way") and to test how robust that hypothesis is.

The simulation never produces a result /about the interviewed field/. Its value is
strictly heuristic: it makes an implicit relational claim explicit, contrastable and
falsifiable. Used this way it is legitimate; used as a forecast it is not.

* The decisive rule: keep the empirical / fiction boundary visible

The whole method stands or falls on one distinction, which every figure and every
sentence in the write-up must respect.

- *Empirical layer (what the interviews license).* A 5-7 interview study cannot
  populate a census, and that is not its job. What it /can/ do well is *profile an
  instance* — its "way of working". This is the empirical core and it belongs to the
  instance, not to a population.

- *Fictitious layer (what is stipulated).* The /population/ on which the instance acts
  is built as a small set of theoretically-motivated *ideal-types*. These are not data;
  they are a probe, exactly as the project already treats its thin author cohort
  ("probe, not census"; see the Usage and Methodology documents). This precedent is
  what authorises the move.

If this line is sharp, the exercise is rigorous. If it is blurred, the twin becomes a
machine for confirming any intuition.

* Role allocation

| Element                         | Source                  | Status      |
|---------------------------------+-------------------------+-------------|
| Instance "way of working"       | the interviews          | empirical   |
| Population of actors            | ideal-types you stipulate | fictitious |
| Trend × transformation × horizon | your hypothesis         | argument    |
| Output (reciprocity, inequality, crossing) | the model     | if-then     |

The interviews drive the *instance profile*; the stipulated population drives the
*demography*; your hypothesis is the *transformation* you apply; the twin returns the
*relational consequence*.

* Step by step

** 1. Profile the instance from the interviews

From the interview material, extract — in Theory-of-the-Relation terms — the following,
and nothing the interviews do not support:

- *Type.* Is the instance a *control* instance (it regulates, selects, certifies, sets
  a threshold) or a *mediation* instance (it carries, represents, translates a crossing)?
- *Location.* In which relational structure does it act — A (Culture), B (Politics),
  C (Economy), D (Media)? An instance may straddle two (e.g. an art-market expert acts
  in A and gates the crossing into D).
- *Target.* Which /sequence/ within that structure does it favour or suppress?
- *Selectivity over time.* How strong is its regulation, and is it tightening or
  loosening? This becomes a *force curve* (=poids=, 0-1) across a few dated points.

Write each instance as one or more rows of a parc file
(=aid,annee,structure,categorie,sequence,poids=); flag control instances with
=categorie=controle=. Each interview informant typically maps to one such instance or
to one feature of its curve.

** 2. Stipulate the type-population

Build a small population of actors as ideal-types, each motivated by your case, not by
data. Express each as a contract table (=aid,annee,structure,categorie,sequence=): an
actor holds a position from one dated stage to the next. Concentrate them where your
case lives, give them a status gradient (etabli / aspirant / decu / conservateur), and
let only a documented few cross into another structure. Keep the population small and
transparent — it is a probe.

** 3. Load and detect trends

Load the cohort as the population and the parc as the instances. Click *extract
trends*: the twin derives them deterministically from the whole loaded history. You
should see presence trends (where the actors are and where they cross) and control
trends (where the instances tighten). These are the handles for the scenario.

** 4. State the hypothesis as a transformation

Pick the trend that embodies your instance's action (e.g. "A control rising"), choose
the *transformation* that names your hypothesis, set its *magnitude* and *horizon*:

- *accelerate* — the instance tightens / becomes more selective;
- *dampen* — it loosens;
- *reverse* — it inverts its tendency;
- *freeze* — it stops where it is;
- *redirect X→Y* — it diverts the action toward another structure (e.g. A→D: gating
  toward media visibility);
- *continue* — the faithful baseline.

Compute: the timeline extends by the horizon and plays the projection; the readout
contrasts the starting state with the projection and states the consequence.

** 5. Read the output as an if-then, contrastively

Interpret *differences*, never levels. Three readings carry the argument:

- *channel* (reciprocity of the target): does the instance open or close the crossing?
- *inequality* (transversal Gini): does it concentrate or spread?
- *stratification* (status Gini of the target structure): does the apex homogenise and
  the base stratify?

Export the figure (*⬇ SVG*); its subtitle already records trend × transformation ×
horizon, and a dashed marker shows the pivot year.

* Guardrails (non-negotiable for the write-up)

1. *Contrastive, not absolute.* Compare two ways of working on the same population, or
   the same instance across several populations. The comparison is the finding; the raw
   numbers are not.
2. *Sensitivity.* Vary the stipulated population and show the qualitative conclusion
   holds. If it collapses when an invented parameter moves, it is an artefact — and that
   is itself a valuable lesson.
3. *Strict labelling.* Every figure carries "fictitious scenario — heuristic value". No
   number is reported as data about the interviewed field.
4. *What the twin does not model.* If the instance's real lever is, say, /price/
   (Preisbildung), the twin does not produce the price. The price is your
   /operationalisation/ of the instance's work; the model returns its /relational
   consequence/ on circulation — selectivity, stratification, reciprocity, crossing.
   State this explicitly, or you fall into the "projection trap" (Methodology document):
   reading back out of the simulation only what you put in.

* Worked example: the "Auktion" art market

Files (generated by =scripts/generators/gen_auktion_example.py=):
=examples/auktion-art-market/data/auktion_artists_cohort.csv= and
=auktion_control_parc.csv=.

*Case.* A student interviews experts at auction houses (=Auktionen=) in Leipzig (East)
and the Rhineland / Dortmund (West) who appraise and sell art. In TR terms these
experts are *control instances* straddling A (Culture) and D (Media): by making the
price they gate which works circulate, accumulate prestige and cross into media
visibility, and thereby regulate the circulation of regional artists in A.

*Empirical layer (the parc).* Three control instances — =auktion_leipzig= (regional,
moderate), =auktion_rheinland= (stronger, more selective), =auktion_media_house= (gates
the crossing into D) — plus two mediation instances (=galleries=, =critics_press=) that
carry the crossing. Their force curves are the "way of working" a real interview study
would profile.

*Fictitious layer (the population).* Eighteen artists in four ideal-types: contemporary
renowned (living blue-chip), deceased former-established, deceased risen in prestige
(rediscovered), and aspiring regional (the ones whose circulation is regulated).
Concentrated in A, with a thin documented crossing into D.

*What the twin detects* from these two files: /D presence rising/ (artists reaching
media visibility), /A control rising/ and /D control rising/ (the houses tightening),
and /A presence falling/ (the regulated regional artists thinning). These are precisely
the handles for the hypotheses.

*Example hypotheses to test.*

- "If the Western house tightens selection, the channel for regional aspirants narrows
  and the apex of A homogenises." → trend /A control rising/, transformation
  *accelerate*, high magnitude. Read reciprocity (channel) and stratification (apex).
- "If the houses divert their gatekeeping toward media prestige, the A→D crossing
  widens for the few and closes for the many." → transformation *redirect A→D*.
- "If regulation loosens, regional circulation re-opens." → *dampen*. Compare against
  the *accelerate* run on the same population — the /difference/ is the argument.

* The inverse case (equally legitimate, more native to the model)

Instead of holding the instance and varying the population, interview *actors situated
in the relational orders* (artists, gallerists, curators) to profile them empirically,
and *stipulate the mediation instances* as the fictitious, transformable layer. This
mobilises the central A↔D mediation channel of the Theory of the Relation directly. The
discipline is identical: interviews profile the better-documented side (here the
actors), the other side is the variable you transform, and the output is an if-then.

The symmetry is the point. In one design you hold the instance and vary the population;
in the other you hold the population and vary the instance. In both, the boundary
between what is profiled and what is stipulated stays visible — and the scenario remains
a heuristic for sharpening hypotheses, not a prediction.
