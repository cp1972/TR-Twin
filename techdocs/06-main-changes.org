- *v1–v11:* Construction of the base model — relational structures and sequences, chains, regime, pump/feedback/mirroring, inter-structure coupling, Gini, aesthetics.
- *v12:* Simplified chronology (always keyframe-driven), speed control, sequences arranged in a ring around the core.
- *v13:* Progressive (smoothed) rupture instead of abrupt disintegration; geometric speed control; CSV import.
- *v14:* Migration of actors (mass-conserving), sclerosis condition for the structure switch.
- *v15:* Reproducible random seed; visualisation of migration (golden dots).

- *v18:* Increased hysteresis margin against regime flickering (which previously stifled the dynamism).
- *v19:* Demographics per relational structure (weight + composition), anchoring, A/B/C/D selection, new CSV format.
- *v20:* Relational dynamism principle (influence to the most mobile relational structure), weaker anchoring and relaxation — the relational size can now deviate strongly from the population.

- *v22:* Non-deviant default values per control (displayed), symmetric start state; tooltips unified in French.
- *v23:* Flight of the disappointed (migration of the fourth category); reciprocity as a measured curve, shown together with inequality in the central panel.
- *v24:* Refined defaults of the healthy state: declassing 0.5 and "advantage for dynamic structures" 0.5 (active mobility, but without effect in the symmetric start).
- *v25:* Mediation instances, stage 1: force per type (relation/sequence/sub-sequence), fed by the processed actor flow; strengthening, weakening, destruction; effect on reciprocity; health display per structure.
- *v26:* Stage 2: power matrix — the health of each structure is the weighted mean of its instances, so that each structure becomes vulnerable where its strength lies.
- *v27:* Stage 3: degraded relay — structural problem in a cascade, strengthening then exhaustion of the taking-over instance (energy reserve), control instance that re-regulates; display of the "unregulated tension" that diminishes reciprocity.
- *v28:* Physical fate, descending coupling: mediation dampens or accelerates the fatigue (factor gm). Good mediation carries, unregulated tension accelerates the disintegration.
- *v29:* Ascending coupling — profiled forces (PT), ascending coupling (deliberately weak in C), threshold expansion (Pareto), control that solves the remainder. The four structures diverge for the first time; the perfect state is preserved (threshold 1.15).
- *v30:* Display of the three instance forces per structure (relation/sequence/sub-sequence) instead of a capped mean; force ceiling raised from 1.7 to 2.5, so that the lower instance is no longer artificially plateaued — the profiles (especially those of C) become legible and respond to the controls.
- *v31:* Fourth display bar per structure for the engagement of control above its rest value (high in D with simultaneously low tension — the evidence of successful regulation).
- *v32:* Satellisation I: the distress of marginalisation. Satellisation (deficit × dominance) feeds the structural problem (the cascade and control of the marginalised structures kick in) and erodes the internal solidity (reversible). Reciprocity and inequality are now read two-sidedly — no longer only from the perspective of expansion.
- *v33:* Satellisation II (active): absorption (capped by a lower bound) and continuous orbit of the satellite toward the dominant; ruler debouncing, which resolves the limit cycle driven by ruler flickering (end of the visible jumping of the relational structures).
- *v34:* Reorganisation of the interface: central evaluation panel at the top left (evolution curve, global bars, instances per structure); title "Theory of the Relation. Digital Twin"; removal of the "Shake/Draw" buttons and of the separate trajectory.
- *v35:* Permeability between structures: explicit diffusion of the inner composition toward the cross-structural mean (KDIFF = 1.5), whereby the control "over" acts visibly; zero at the perfect state.
- *v36:* Terminological unification of the interface: "structure (relationnelle)" instead of "structure", "séquence" instead of "sous-structure", "fragments séquences" instead of "morceau"; the misleading control "internal circulation" is clarified to "assimilation".
- *v37:* Fine-tuning of the labels: control "actors circulation" (rate, distinguished from "migration of actors"); legend of the mini-curve restored (Gini white / reciprocity turquoise); the red bar "tension" becomes "overload".

The central panel is now a horizontal strip of three blocks (global / instances / per structure).
- *v40:* Controller profiles: the twelve mechanism controls can be governed over time via a CSV (linear interpolation, label as alias), governed controls marked turquoise. Verified headless: correct interpolation, a neutral profile reproduces the manual run, time-varying controls change the dynamism stably, the perfect state is preserved.

- *v60:* What-if scenarios ("et si"), stage 1: a no-code scenario panel. Trends are derived /deterministically/ from the loaded history (presence from the structural shares, control from the capacity floor — read directly from the drivers, no stochastic replay). A scenario is a historical trend × a transformation chosen from a trilingual verb menu (continue / accelerate / dampen / reverse / freeze / redirect X→Y) × a horizon. A deterministic readout contrasts the starting state (last historical year) with the projection at the horizon and states the consequence in plain language (channel widens/narrows, inequality up/down, stratification up/down). Scenarios can be saved as cards and replayed. Guiding principle: a scenario = historical trend × transformation × horizon, never code.
- *v61:* What-if scenarios, stage 2 — the scenario plays on the timeline. The timeline is extended by the horizon and playback runs from a few years before the pivot into the projected tail (warm-started so the simulation state is correct at the entry year), so the structures visibly move. Magnitude and horizon are now effective levers (previously indexed on the faint historical slope, below display precision). A reset button restores the historical frieze; the readout and the cards move to the central panel, beside the circulation flows. Verified headless: frieze extends, magnitude and horizon change the projection, control scenario, determinism, reset, card replay, trilingual.

- *v64:* Empty relational structures in the data mode, and a stacked information panel. - *v65:* Stimulus protocol and the satellisation mechanisms M1–M3. (1) A stimulus is a deliberate change of /one/ relational structure at its core sequence, for a set duration — the heart of a structure, from which its butterfly path and its expansion or shrinking are decided. /Strengthen/ reinforces the core sequence g[x][x] by k·dt per step (the operation of holding a circle pressed, k = 2.6 by default). /Weaken/ moves k·dt from the leading sequence R = ruler[x] to its successor in the order A→B→C→D→A: once the successor leads, the structure is reprogrammed around it and runs along that sequence's butterfly path, as if it were that sequence's structure; weakening further moves the newly set sequence on (weakening A → around B; weakening D → around A, then around B). The stimulus tests whether the targeted change prevents the return to the state reached without it; it does not show which structure rules. (2) Three mechanisms of the theory, each switchable and off by default: /M1/ the chains of a satellised structure align with the sequential order of the satelliser (its path without the satellite's own sequence, rate × satellisation; the satellite's own path plasticity is scaled by 1 − satellisation; chains fixed by a profile stay fixed); /M2/ a structure satellised above 0.5 for 2.0 time units runs entirely under the satelliser's sequence (ruler = satelliser), released below 0.2; /M3/ the satelliser's instance types strengthen the same types in its satellites, weighted by the square of their strength relative to its strongest instance (the strongest leads, the weaker are drawn in). (3) A panel "stimulus protocol" (structure, direction, duration, strength, M1–M3, apply now, Σ stimulus probe) and the headless functions =reizSetzen=, =reizStop=, =setMech=, =reizLauf=, =reizProbe= and =reizSchwelle= (see 04). A probe compares the stimulus run with the control run of the same length; verdict returned, set, or still moving (sizes changing by 0.005 or more over the last 1000 steps). Default free run 6000 steps: with 1500 free steps slow transients can be mistaken for set states. Verified headless: without a stimulus and with M1–M3 off, the end states and stimulus probes of all 28 profiles × 8 seeds of the TR pipeline are identical to v64; the pipeline's stimulus probe computed with =reizLauf= reproduces its v64 values exactly. Observed with the TR profile: weakening at strength 2.6 moves the leading sequence on about every 55 steps, so a long weakening turns the structure through the whole order and the outcome depends on the moment the stimulus ends.
- *v66:* Return to the own structure: weakening along the path of the leading structure, and three further mechanisms of satellisation, each switchable and off by default. (1) /Weakening/ no longer follows the fixed order A→B→C→D→A, which after a full turn brought a weakened structure back to its own core sequence. The leading sequence of the weakened structure now passes its share to the next sequence along the butterfly path of the structure that rules most or gains most power in the relational space (largest size plus 2 × its smoothed growth); once the structure runs under that structure's core sequence, weakening further takes from its own core and gives to the leader's. The weakening itself never brings a structure back to its own core. Without a clear leader (lead below 0.01, as in the symmetric state) the order A→B→C→D→A applies, skipping the structure's own sequence. (2) /M4, control of the satellite:/ the control instances of a satellised structure strengthen its own core sequence (rate 0.8 × satellisation × own control). They can be satellised in turn — a share of alienated control grows with the satellisation and the relative control strength of the satelliser — but at most to 80 %, so that the structure keeps itself as a structure; alienated control no longer resolves the structure's own residue. A takeover (M2) is released only while the own control is not mostly alienated (below 0.5). (3) /M5, saturation of domination (Pareto):/ the dominant structure accumulates a saturation that grows with its satellites and over-proportionally with every further gain beyond the even share (a marginal gain in domination costs more resources in networking the others than no gain); the time scale is the setting "saturation time" (default 40 model time units). Saturation erodes the structure in favour of the others, the stronger ones first, and a saturated dominant absorbs less; the erosion lasts only until another structure is clearly stronger (by 0.03), so that domination passes on rather than collapsing to a quarter; saturation recovers once the structure no longer rules. Takeovers by a satelliser that no longer dominates are released. (4) /M6, force of control (DiMaggio):/ per structure, ½ the diversification of its instances (evenness of relation, sequence, sub-sequence and control) plus ½ its networking (cross-structural circulation); with weak force a structure is absorbed more easily, its control resolves less, and its control is alienated faster. (5) The stimulus run records the changes of domination in the free run (=verlauf=). Verified headless: with all mechanisms off and no stimulus, the pipeline's end states and stimulus probes are identical to v64. Observed with the TR profile (seed 1, politics strengthened for 300 steps, M1–M6): politics rises to 61 %, its saturation reaches 1 after about 9000 steps, the economy rises to 32 % without overtaking, and afterwards all structures run around their own sequences again while politics declines slowly (33 % after 39 000 steps); with M1–M3 only, politics stays at 68 %.
- *v67:* The mechanisms M1–M6 belong to a theory, like its chains. The Theory of the Relation has all six: they are on in the default configuration (no profile loaded, and after "defaults"). A slider profile declares the mechanisms of its theory in a row =mechanismen,M1,M2,…= (also =mechanisms=, =mecanismes=), and optionally the saturation time in a row =saettigungsdauer,40=; a profile without such a row has none — a theory that does not claim them does not get them, as a profile without chain rows does not change the coupling order of the theory it encodes. The import status shows the mechanisms in force, the checkboxes follow the profile and can still be changed by hand. Consequence: the default TR configuration no longer computes as v64 (with M1–M6 off it still does, bit for bit); profiles without a mechanism row compute as before. The standard chains are unchanged (economy B→D→A→C, book p. 105).
- *v68:* Runs from files. A run file (key and value; English, German or French) names the mode, the theory profiles or the contract table (and instances), the time axis, the seeds, a stimulus and the trend window. The interface and the command line (=node run.js <run file>=, with =package.json= for =npm install=) call the same functions; a run is computed with fixed steps, and the animation replays the same step sequence, so that what is shown is what is computed (the live animation of v67 advanced by the frame time and was not reproducible). Results — simulation mode: the assumptions of the theories next to the TR (an undeclared slider is marked as running with the TR value), and the results over the seeds with the difference to the TR: inequalities, reciprocity, sizes, domination, satellisation, regimes and their changes, the stimulus verdict; data mode: trends per period (10 years by default, up to 100), detail per structure, circulation × mobility (category changes one at a time; jumps of several categories counted as steps, those beyond the first marked as inferred), flows, permeability. Report (HTML), tables, series and figures for download. The right column is simplified: keyframes, population data, the separate import fields, exports, the what-if scenarios and the stimulus panel leave the interface (their code is kept); sliders, chains, seed and prism probes move read-only into a collapsed section for advanced users. With the same settings v68 computes bit-identically to v67's deterministic paths (=tests/test_v67_equal.js=, =tests/test_stimulus.js=); a run does not depend on what was computed before it (=tests/test_repeat.js=); interface and command line agree (=tests/test_gui_cli.py=). Fix: a contract-table record keeps whether the table gives a category (needed for mobility); the mechanics are unchanged. After the first tests: the animation starts by itself after the computation; the legend of the structures, the social composition and the sequence detail leave the right column; the run file and its files can be chosen one after the other; simulation mode gains a table and a figure of circulation and mobility (read from the migration of the twin, without acting on it), the sizes of the sequences per structure, a heatmap of who satellises whom, the trajectories of horizontal and vertical inequality and of the reciprocity of each structure; trajectories are drawn as lines with the range as a band, with readable axes. Then, in both modes, the stability over the seeds: a table per seed (outcome as the sequence of dominant structures and the regimes at the end, sizes, satellisation, inequalities, course of the domination, circulation), the satellisation force of each structure per seed, a stability table and figures; a domination shorter than 300 steps, in a run over years shorter than 10 years (before: 300 computing steps, about 50 years), belongs to the domination around it. Thresholds of domination for every slider (run-file key =thresholds=, on by default), the stimulus probe with each structure weakened (=stimulus probe=, on by default), findings for the interpretation under three questions, and an interpretation file named by the run file (=interpretation=) that goes into the report (=tests/test_thresholds.js=); the direction /stärken/ is read in a stimulus.
