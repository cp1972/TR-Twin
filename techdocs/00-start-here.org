#+TITLE: Start here: what the twin is, what it assumes, what can be done with it
#+OPTIONS: toc:t num:t ^:nil
#+LANGUAGE: en

This document presupposes neither the Theory of the Relation, nor the book in
which it is set out, nor any acquaintance with the model. It is intended as the
entry point; the nine documents that follow go into detail and assume that this
one has been read.

* What this is

A digital twin is an executable representation of something, held close enough
to the original that experiments can be carried out on the representation
rather than on the thing itself. The original is usually a technical system.
Here it is a sociological theory.

The Theory of the Relation advances claims about how societies cohere and come
apart: that actors circulate between domains of social life, that institutions
channel this circulation, and that inequalities arise from its unevenness.
These claims are formulated in prose. A prose argument can be coherent in its
exposition and still contain tensions that become apparent only when its
propositions are required to operate simultaneously.

The theory was therefore reconstructed as a program. Each claim was rendered as
a mechanism and each concept as a quantity. Running the model shows what
follows from the theory's own premises once they are made to interact, and
where a consequence appears implausible the mechanism responsible can be
located. This is the first use of the twin: an examination of the theory's
internal coherence.

Two further uses emerged in the course of the work and have proved at least as
consequential. Alternative theoretical frameworks can be encoded in the same
machinery, so that their divergences become observable rather than merely
assertable. And empirical data can be supplied to the model, which then reads
the state of a social field from them. All three are described below.

The twin is a single file, =tr-twin.html=, which opens in any current browser.
It requires no installation, no server and no account.

* A note on the mode of construction

The model was developed in collaboration with an artificial intelligence. The
theory, the sociological judgements and the decisions as to what constitutes a
faithful translation from concept to mechanism are the author's; a substantial
part of the implementation, of the headless verification and of this
documentation was written with AI assistance.

This is recorded for three reasons. It is a fact about the provenance of the
code and belongs in the open. It accounts for the density of verification
described in the sections that follow: each mechanism was tested headless
before entering the application, a practice whose cost falls considerably when
the test harness itself can be generated. And it is a claim the reader may wish
to examine, for which purpose both the code and the verification scripts are
included in this repository.

* How the twin operates

** Four relational structures

The model divides social life into four *relational structures*. In the
interface these are labelled Culture (A), Politics (B), Economy (C) and Media
(D). The labels are an applied interpretation, adopted because they render
empirical material legible; the theory derives the four structures more
abstractly, from a single distinction — whether a domain organises itself
around the *positions* actors hold or around the *exchanges* they enter — and
from its first- and second-order deviations. For present purposes it is
sufficient that there are four, that they differ in kind rather than in
content, and that each carries the features of the other three in subordinate
form.

Each structure has a *size*, understood as its weight within the social field
as a whole. The four sizes are normalised to sum to one, so that a structure
can grow only at the expense of the others. This is not merely a convenience of
implementation: it encodes the claim that relational weight is a distributional
quantity.

** Sequences

Each structure is internally articulated into four *sequences* — modes of
operating within a domain: its core, its mode of representation, its mode of
production, and its mode of circulation and control. There are sixteen
sequences in all.

The structures differ in the *order* in which circulation traverses their
sequences. This order, termed the chain in the model, is what distinguishes one
structure from another; altering it alters the structure's character.

** Actor categories

Actors are not represented individually. Each sequence holds a distribution
over four categories:

- *Established* — mobile through conquest; sufficiently free of structural
  constraint to leave one domain for another. They circulate most.
- *Aspirants* — consolidating a position, and therefore moving within their
  domain rather than out of it.
- *Conservatives* — defending a position undergoing devaluation. Over time they
  tend to lose it and pass into the fourth category.
- *Disappointed* — mobile through disappointment; they too depart, but by way
  of declassing rather than of advancement.

The two most mobile categories occupy the extremes of the distribution rather
than one end of it. Mobility in this model is accordingly not an indicator of
success, and readings that treat it as one will misconstrue the output.

** Mediation instances

*Instances* are the institutions that admit actors, socialise them and sanction
them — schools, editorial boards, professional bodies, funding committees. They
do not circulate; only actors do. An instance gains force as it successfully
processes actors and loses force when it fails to.

They operate at three reaches — the structure as a whole, a single sequence,
and the transitions between sequences — alongside a fourth kind, *control*,
which does not address problems directly but restores the other instances when
they become exhausted.

** What the model computes

Two measures carry most of the interpretative weight:

- *Reciprocity*, understood as the fluidity of circulation between structures.
  It is high where actors can move and be received, and falls where a structure
  closes or where one structure absorbs the others. It is measured rather than
  set: the interface provides no reciprocity control.
- *Inequality*, computed as a Gini coefficient at three nested levels
  simultaneously — between structures (transversal), between sequences within a
  structure (horizontal), and between actor categories within a sequence
  (vertical).

The three levels do not move in concert, and their divergence is generally more
informative than any of them taken singly.

* What the model assumes

The results a model yields are purchased with its assumptions. Those made here
are set out below. They need not be accepted; but they are operative whenever
the twin is used, and should therefore be visible.

1. *The primacy of circulation.* Society is conceived not as an arrangement of
   positions that actors occupy but as a set of movements they undertake, with
   position understood as a sediment of circulation rather than as its ground.
   This constitutes the theory's principal departure from Bourdieu, and it is
   built into the mechanics: the model moves actors and derives structure from
   the movement.

2. *Four structures.* The number follows from one distinction together with its
   first- and second-order deviations. Where a domain of empirical material
   appears to belong to none of the four, the model requires that this be
   argued rather than accommodated by the addition of a fifth.

3. *Instances do not circulate.* Only actors do. An institution that appears to
   operate across several structures is therefore either anchored in one
   structure, its reach carried by the actors it has socialised, or else a
   collective designation for several distinct instances. The model admits only
   the second reading. This is a substantive constraint, and in some
   applications an inconvenient one.

4. *Strength becomes vulnerability beyond an optimum.* A structure's dominant
   instance grows by processing actors, which in turn enlarges the structure —
   a process that remains viable up to a threshold, beyond which the same
   growth produces sclerosis. The mechanism follows Pareto's notion of
   ophelimity; its consequence is that structures become vulnerable at the
   point of their greatest strength rather than at that of their weakness.

5. *Deficits are relayed, not averaged.* Where an instance fails, the next
   narrower one assumes the load and is in turn exhausted, since it operates at
   a level too local to resolve a structural problem. The load descends until
   control intervenes. The model contains no compensation term: a deficit at
   one level is not offset by strength at another.

6. *Internal legitimacy is distinct from systemic reciprocity.* The two are
   separate quantities and may move in opposite directions. A domain whose
   institutions function effectively may be admitting many actors and
   distributing them across stable and unequal ranks. The model keeps the two
   apart deliberately, and conflating them in interpretation is a frequent
   error.

7. *The symmetric state is inert.* At start-up the four structures are of equal
   size, inequality is zero and reciprocity is one. Every mechanism is written
   so as to have no effect at that state. This is a methodological constraint
   rather than a presentational one: any effect observed in a run can be traced
   to an asymmetry that was introduced deliberately.

8. *Runs are deterministic.* A fixed seed governs the initial perturbation, so
   that identical settings yield identical trajectories. The twin is a
   comparative instrument and not a stochastic simulation; determinism secures
   reproducibility but bears on validity not at all.

* What can be done with it

** Without empirical data

A theoretical framework — Parsons, Luhmann, Habermas, White, Tilly, Bourdieu
and others — can be encoded as a set of cursor settings expressing how that
framework construes circulation, mediation and reciprocity. The run is then
compared against the reference state, and the divergence constitutes the
framework's signature within this machinery.

Twenty-four such profiles are supplied in
[[file:../simulation-mode/README.org][simulation-mode/]]. A qualification
belongs here: a divergence is a property of the encoding as much as of the
framework encoded. The profiles are interpretations, and their construction is
documented so that it can be contested. A comparison of this kind is best read
as an argument rendered precise rather than as a measurement of a theory.

** With a small number of cases

This is the situation the twin was extended to accommodate, and the one most
frequently encountered in qualitative sociology and in student research: a
handful of interviews, a dozen biographies, a single archive.

*Prism mode* reads the field through one to five actors treated as *probes* —
trajectories from which the state of the field they traversed may be inferred:
which channels stood open to them, at which points they were arrested, which
instances certified them. A single trajectory under-determines the field, and
the method makes no claim to the contrary; a well-chosen set, however, should
reproduce in miniature what a full cohort exhibits. Where it does not, the
discrepancy is itself informative, and the appropriate first step is to
re-examine the selection of probes rather than to revise the reading of the
field.

See [[file:07-prism-mode-actor-probes.org][document 7]] for the method and
[[file:08-scenario-protocol-for-students.org][document 8]] for a protocol
leading from a small number of interviews to a defensible scenario without
overstating the evidential weight of the sample.

** With a full dataset

A cohort may be supplied as a table — actor, year, structure, category,
sequence — together with the mediation and control instances traversed. The
twin then reads the three levels of inequality from the data and displays their
movement over time.

The worked example in [[file:../data-mode/README.org][data-mode/]] applies this
to 163 English working-class autobiographers between 1790 and 1945. Its coding
pipeline is documented in full, including the residue that the coding was
unable to resolve.

* Reading what the model produces

The twin returns curves, indices and end states. These are artefacts of the
model as much as they are findings about the world, and their interpretation
calls for some care.

*The reference state is a construction, not a norm.* The symmetric start state
serves as a baseline against which deviations can be measured. It is not a
description of a desirable society, and departures from it are not defects.

*Outputs are conditional on settings.* Every trajectory is the trajectory of a
particular configuration. Comparisons are informative between runs that differ
in one respect and are otherwise identical; the discipline of varying a single
element at a time applies here as elsewhere.

*Reproducibility is not validity.* The determinism of the model guarantees that
a result can be regenerated. It carries no implication as to whether the result
corresponds to anything outside the model.

*The structure labels are interpretative.* Culture, Politics, Economy and Media
constitute one reading of four abstract structures. Empirical material that
does not sort readily into them can still be analysed, but the mapping must
then be constructed and defended explicitly.

*A measure is not yet a finding.* A Gini coefficient of 0.37 is a quantity
produced under stated assumptions. What it licenses one to say about the
material depends on the coding decisions upstream of it, which is why the
worked example documents those decisions at length.

* What the twin does not do

- It does not forecast. Scenarios extend a trajectory under stated assumptions
  and are to be read as arguments rendered explicit rather than as predictions.
- It does not yield individual-level results. Actors enter as distributions;
  even in prism mode a trajectory is evidence concerning the field rather than
  concerning the person.
- It does not establish whether the Theory of the Relation is true. It bears on
  the theory's coherence and renders its consequences inspectable.
- It does not dispense with sociological judgement at any point — neither in
  the construction of a profile, nor in the coding of a corpus, nor in the
  reading of an output.
