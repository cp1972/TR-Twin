* Before you start

This document sets out the mechanisms of the model and the quantities they
operate on. It is the reference for anyone who needs to know precisely what the
twin computes, and it presupposes the vocabulary introduced in
[[file:00-start-here.org][document 0]] and developed in
[[file:01-theory-of-the-relation-purpose-and-concepts.org][document 1]].

Three points of orientation may assist a first reading. The mapping table below
is the pivot of the whole documentation: every quantity in the model is there
assigned to the theoretical concept it is intended to render, and any mechanism
described later can be traced back through it. The sections on the mathematical
model proceed from the general to the particular and need not be read in order;
each states the quantity it governs and the theoretical claim it implements.
And the recurring notes marked *verified headless* record that a mechanism was
tested outside the browser before it entered the application — these are
records of what was checked, and they indicate the boundaries within which the
behaviour described has actually been observed.

A caution that applies throughout: the mechanisms below are formulated so as to
have no effect at the symmetric reference state. Where a section describes a
force, a threshold or a coupling, it describes something that remains inert
until an asymmetry is introduced.

* Mapping: theory → digital twin

The following table maps the central concepts of the theory onto the quantities and mechanisms of the digital twin. The concrete relational-structure labels (Culture, Politics, Economy, Media) are an applied interpretation of the four abstract relational structures, chosen to make real data interpretable — they are not fixed by the theory.

* Architecture of the digital twin

The state of the model is described at each instant by the following quantities:

- *S[4]* — the relational size (weight of influence) of each relational structure; normalised, sum = 1, conserved.
- *g[4][4]* — the size of each sequence within its relational structure; each row normalised to 1. g[x][x] is the core (self-replica).
- *act[4][4][4]* — the composition of the four actor categories in each of the 16 sequences (64 variables, each normalised).
- *WT[4][4]* — the attraction matrix (plastic chains); each row normalised. It encodes the sequential order of each relational structure.
- *ruler[4]* — the current regime of each relational structure (which sequence dominates; autonomous if the self-replica reigns, otherwise satellite).
- *F[4]* — the fatigue store of each relational structure (the basis of rupture).
- *Demographics per relational structure* — a population weight and a composition per relational structure, each as a time series (frame-to-frame interpolated).

* The mathematical model

The model advances in small time steps. A global speed factor scales the time step; all mechanisms are formulated continuously, so that no abrupt jumps arise. In what follows, the mechanisms are described in the order of their interaction.

** Viability and regime

The viability of a sequence follows a bell curve over its size: too small is not viable, too large likewise not; an adjustable optimum lies in between. The regime of a relational structure is that sequence with the highest viability (with hysteresis, so that the regime does not flicker under close conditions). If the self-replica reigns, the relational structure is autonomous (actor-centred); if another sequence reigns, the relational structure is a satellite (exchange-centred) of that other one.

** Chains and plasticity

Each relational structure possesses a chain — an ordered succession of its sequences (sequential order). Along this chain, circulation flows toward the reigning terminal. The chains are plastic: the attraction matrix WT slowly relaxes toward reachable targets, so that the sequential order can reorganise itself over time.

** Actor categories and vertical mobility

In each sequence, actors rise and fall between the four categories according to "fortune" (a mixture of the viability of the sequence and the size of the relational structure). High fortune drives the ascent toward the possessing, low fortune the descent toward the excluded. At the same time the composition relaxes gently toward the prescribed demographics of the relational structure. The mobility of a sequence — its share of circulating actors — is carried strongly by the possessing, more weakly by the aspirants.

** Circulation: pump, feedback, mirroring

Within each relational structure a "pump" transports mass along the chain toward the terminal, weighted by the mobility of the actors involved; a feedback redistributes a part back. Between the relational structures, reciprocity acts as mirroring: the sequences mirror the sizes of the other relational structures, so that the relational structures mutually influence one another.

** Inter-structure coupling, drainage, and permeability

The excluded drive the drainage (Entleerung): a sequence releases the more mass to the corresponding sequences of the other relational structures, the greater its share of excluded. Thus features of one relational structure spread across society, and a strong core radiates into the other relational structures.

*Permeability (diffusion of the inner composition).* Beyond the drainage driven by the excluded, an explicit diffusion acts between the structures: each sequence is drawn toward the cross-structural mean of its column, to the degree over · KDIFF (KDIFF = 1.5). High permeability homogenises the inner textures — the structures become alike (the same sequences) — while low permeability lets each keep its own texture. Verified headless: between over = 0 and over = 2.5 the cross-structural dispersion of the sequences falls from ~0.098 to ~0.012. The operation preserves the per-structure normalisation (the pull toward the column mean does not change the row sum) and is zero at the perfect, symmetric state. The control is named "Permeability between structures" in the interface.

** Migration of actors

Migration is formulated as a mass flow and is fully conserving. It concerns above all the possessing (the circulating category):

- *Possessing in the self-replica (x,x)* advance one step in the chain of their relational structure and remain in the same relational structure (accumulation of advantages).
- *Possessing in another sequence (x,y):* the majority advance in the chain; a minority dare the switch into the relational structure y itself — into its reigning core — in order to maximise the advantage of their position (for instance the scientist who, via the "politics of science", switches into politics).
- *Sclerosis condition:* this switch succeeds only if the target structure is not sclerosed (core not over-powerful, not in rupture). Otherwise it fails, and the migrants stay.

*Declassing on arrival:* whoever switches into a foreign relational structure arrives declassed — they must resocialise. Their category is lowered on entry; if resocialisation succeeds (good fortune of the target structure), they rise again, otherwise they sink further (successive declassing). A control governs the level of these resocialisation costs.

*Flight of the disappointed (fourth category).* Not only the established circulate: the disappointed too gradually leave their relational structure. A disappointed actor of the sequence (x, a) migrates into the relational structure a — that relational structure in which their sequence is the core sequence — the structure whose self-replica that sequence is — and arrives there "right at the bottom". If the switch succeeds (target structure not sclerosed), it relieves the abandoned relational structure and its mediation instances; if it is blocked, circulation backs up (friction). Freely possible out-migration thus reduces the questioning of the relational structure and maintains circulation; only the prevented flight is a sign of disturbed reciprocity.

** Fatigue of dominance and rupture

An over-endowed relational structure builds up fatigue. If this exceeds a threshold, a progressive rupture sets in: the relational structure gradually releases its surplus to the smaller relational structures, its core erodes, the fatigue is reduced. Thus arises — as the theory suggests for monopolies — a cycle of ascent, over-power, and disintegration, without abrupt jumps.

** Demographics per relational structure: weight and composition

Each relational structure carries, over time, two data: a population weight (its size) and a composition (the four categories). The weight anchors the size S of the relational structure — S is drawn gently toward the normalised weight — while the composition governs mobility. A control for the "demographic anchoring" determines how closely the sizes follow the data: with strong anchoring the circle reads as a population, with weak anchoring as relational power.

Important insight: a globally uniform demographics cannot differentiate the relational structures (equal forces on normalised sizes cancel out). Only a structure-specific demographics makes the data effective.

** Relational dynamism principle

So that the relational size of a relational structure can deviate strongly from its population, influence flows to the relational structures with the most mobile composition — independently of their population count. This is formulated conservatively (what the dynamic ones gain, the inert ones lose). Thus, for example, culture, though sparsely populated, can extend its influence over the century far beyond its numerical weight — a core concern of the theory, that relational power does not coincide with number.

** Inequality (Gini)

As a summary measure of the inequality between the relational structures, the model continuously computes a Gini index and shows its trajectory. The finer resolution into three levels — vertical (within a sequence), horizontal (between sequences of a relational structure), and transversal (between the relational structures) — was, up to v58, a planned extension; since v59 it is implemented as a measurement cascade. It is developed in detail below.

** Reciprocity as a measured curve

The theory understands reciprocity not as a control variable but as a product of circulation: as a more or less strong degree of the legitimacy of that circulation, carried by actors and mediation instances. The digital twin therefore measures it as an emergent curve in the range (0, 1] — analogous to the Gini, but complementary: the Gini measures the inequality of the sizes, reciprocity the fluidity and legitimacy of circulation.

It is computed as: reciprocity = (0.5 + 0.5 · instance health) − (0.50 · inequality + 0.30 · friction + 0.10 · rupture + 0.05 · sclerosis) − 0.40 · unresolved remainder, bounded to [0, 1]. Instance health is the size-weighted mean of the mediating health of the structures; the unresolved remainder (meanResid) is the size-weighted share of the structural problems that the cascade, control included, does not solve (shown in the interface as "overload"). Friction is the share of attempted structure switches (established as well as disappointed) that fail at a sclerosed target structure. In the perfect, symmetric state reciprocity is ~1; it falls with inequality, with blocked circulation, and with unresolved overload, but always remains positive.

This clarified theoretically: the free out-migration of the disappointed does not lower reciprocity (it relieves the system), but the blocked out-migration does. Both curves — inequality and reciprocity — are shown in the central panel next to the relational structures, so that their fluctuations become immediately visible together with the movement of the relational structures.

** Morphological and structural determinants: a communicating vessel

The control "weight of number" (demographic anchoring) is more than a display option: it governs the relation between the morphological determinants (population) and the structural determinants (position and circulation) — that relation which, according to the theory, determines the actor categories and the whole system. The two behave like communicating vessels: what the one gains, the other loses.

If the relational order leads (low anchoring), a relational structure draws its weight from its circulation — from the mobility of its actors and its viability — not from its head count. A sparsely populated but dynamic relational structure therefore retains relational weight, and the distributive logic of circulation (pump, migration, coupling) continues to redistribute between the relational structures. Inequality stays low, reciprocity high — the primacy of circulation.

If, by contrast, the demographic order leads (high anchoring), the relational sizes are drawn toward the population distribution. If this population is itself unbalanced — for instance an ageing society with a very low birth rate, which puts under pressure the renewal of the economic sector and the financing of pensions — then this demographic imbalance translates immediately into relational inequality: the distributive logic of circulation is braked by the weight of number, inequality rises, reciprocity falls. It is precisely here that the capacity or incapacity of circulation to maintain the sequences and relational structures is decided.

Two qualifications are essential. First, the control does not change the demographics itself (birth rate, age pyramid) — this is the datum fed in over the time series; the control determines only to what degree this demographic fact may govern the relational order. Second, it acts only if the demographics is itself unbalanced: on a balanced population it remains without effect. Precisely where the data are tense, the choice between morphological and structural determination decides the outcome.

From this it follows immediately how essential it will be to be able to feed not only the demographics but also the controls themselves from real data via a CSV and to vary them over time. Thus the effects on inequality and reciprocity could be observed along the real tensions between social morphology and structural determinants.

** Mediation instances: force, power matrix, and degraded relay

Each structure carries three substantial instance forces (relation, sequence, sub-sequence) and one control force. The three are fed by the actor flow they process at their level: the relation by inter-structure migration, the sequence by the internal pump, the sub-sequence by the flow at the two joints (Established↔Aspirants, Conservatives↔Disappointed). The flows are normalised to a neutral reference value, so that the perfect state keeps the forces at ~1; if the flow dries up, the force falls toward zero — the instance is destroyed.

Power matrix (profile per structure). The health of a structure is the mean of its three instances weighted by the power matrix, so that the dominant instance counts the most (A→relation, B→sequence, C→sub-sequence; control is dominant in D). This weighted health feeds reciprocity: a structure becomes sensitive where its strength lies.

Degraded relay. From the instability of a structure (fatigue, sclerosis) a normalised structural problem P ∈ [0, 1] is formed. It descends in a cascade: each instance solves a capped fraction (suitability ceiling 0.95 / 0.40 / 0.15, scaled with the force) and passes the remainder to the subordinate one. The taking-over instance is first strengthened (its target force rises) but in doing so draws on an energy reserve that is exhausted under sustained overload (rapid activation, slow exhaustion). The remainder left after the sub-sequence calls on control: its force rises with the remainder and restores the energy of the others. The unresolved remainder — the "unregulated tension" — diminishes reciprocity. In the perfect state P = 0, there is no cascade, and reciprocity stays at ~1.

*Completed (v28–v31):* the physical fate of the structure — the complete Pareto loop. How the cascade now actively intervenes in the size and rupture mechanics is described in the following section.

** The physical fate: profiled forces, ascending coupling, threshold expansion, and control

This stage couples mediation to the size and rupture mechanics. Like all preceding ones, it was first calibrated and verified headless (Node.js) and only afterwards taken into the application. Four building blocks mesh.

*** (1) Profiled target forces

Each structure receives a force profile PT: the dominant instance stands at 1, the subordinate ones at 0.45. The health contributing to reciprocity is normalised with min(1, I/PT), so that the perfect state — forces on the respective profile — is preserved and no structure appears favoured merely by its profile.

| Structure | Relation | Sequence | Sub-sequence |
|-----------+----------+----------+--------------|
| A · Culture | 1.00 | 0.45 | 0.45 |
| B · Politics | 0.45 | 1.00 | 0.45 |
| C · Economy | 0.45 | 0.45 | 1.00 |
| D · Media | 0.45 | 0.45 | 0.45 (dominates via control) |

*** (2) Ascending coupling

The surplus of a lower instance over its profile rises upward: max(0, I − PT) of the sub-sequence is added to the target of the sequence, that of the sequence to the target of the relation. The coupling factor LINK = [0.5; 0.6; 0.15; 0.5] (A/B/C/D) is deliberately weak in the economy (C) (0.15): the inertia of small instances whose local strength does not rise — the mathematical correlate of the volatility described in document 1 (Four diverging fates).

*** (3) Threshold expansion (Pareto)

Above a threshold value the dominant instance drives the expansion: ex = KEXP · max(0, dd − 1.15) · S, with KEXP = 0.9 and dd = the value of the dominant instance (relation in A, sequence in B, sub-sequence in C; in D the substantial drive is absent, since control dominates). The threshold 1.15 stabilises the perfect state: below it nothing expands of itself; only the instance driven past the optimum produces expansive pressure — the Pareto loop. Without this threshold even the neutral state would expand (a bug discovered and fixed in v29).

*** (4) Control solves the remainder; coupling to fatigue

Control reduces the remainder left before it: resCtrl = resPre · max(0, 1 − kc · min(1.5; Ictrl)), with kc = 0.45 in D, otherwise 0.18; at the same time it restores the energy of the exhausted instances (ctrlR = 0.45 · Ictrl · resPre, in D with the factor 1.6). The descending coupling to fatigue proceeds via the factor gm = max(0, 1 + KACCEL · RESID − KBUF · RESOLVED) with KACCEL = 1.5 and KBUF = 0.8, which multiplies the growth of fatigue: solved problems (RESOLVED) dampen it, unregulated remainder (RESID) accelerates it.

*Display and ceiling.* So that the lower instance hitting the ceiling in C does not appear artificially capped, the force ceiling was raised from 1.7 to 2.5 (v30). The control panel has since shown the three substantial forces of each structure separately (relation, sequence, sub-sequence) instead of a single, capped mean — for a mean hides precisely the hallmark of C: the strength sitting in the wrong place. Since v31 a fourth bar is added, showing the engagement of control above its rest value.

*Modelling lineage and references.* The saturating, capped force dynamics and the growth up to an optimum stand in the tradition of logistic growth (Verhulst 1838) and the Pareto optimum (Pareto 1909); the relaxation of forces toward a target, the hysteresis of the regime, and the threshold triggering are elementary building blocks of nonlinear dynamics (Strogatz 1994). The control instance, which restores energy and regulates the others, follows the cybernetic notion of a regulator (Ashby 1956). Reciprocity as a systemic measure ties in with the sociological norm of reciprocity (Mauss 1925; Gouldner 1960); inequality is measured via the Gini coefficient (Gini 1912). These references name the provenance of the model's building blocks; they do not claim an exact adoption of the respective original formulas.

** Satellisation: distress of marginalisation, erosion, active satellisation, and ruler debouncing

This stage complements the physical fate with its second pole. Like all preceding ones, it was first calibrated and verified headless (Node.js). Four building blocks and one stabilisation.

*** (1) The degree of satellisation

With the fair share φ = 1/N, the deficit d[x] = max(0, (φ − S[x])/φ) measures how far a structure falls below its share; the dominance dom = max(0, (Smax − φ)/φ) how far the largest exceeds it. Satellisation is their product: sat[x] = min(1, d[x] · dom). Both factors are zero at the symmetric state — satellisation awakens only when a structure is at once shrunk (deficit) and dominated (dominance).

*** (2) Registered distress

Satellisation is fed, with the weight WSAT = 1.0, into the structural problem: P[x] = clamp(1.3·F + 1.2·(g_core − rupt) + WSAT·sat). The marginalised structure thereby receives a problem that engages its cascade and its control; since its instances are weak, a high remainder persists, which enters reciprocity via meanResid. Thus satellisation lowers reciprocity through two channels: the remainder (the satellite cannot regulate itself) and the Gini (the inequality itself).

*** (3) Erosion of internal solidity

The energy of the instances is dampened by KEROD · sat (KEROD = 0.5), in addition to the mere flow shortage. The value is deliberately chosen high: the relation erodes deeply but is never destroyed. As soon as sat falls (liberation), the erosion ceases, the energy recovers (rate 0.13), and the forces rebuild — re-emancipation. Verified headless: after liberation the relation returns from ~0.25 to ~0.69, slowly but completely.

*** (4) Active satellisation: absorption and orbit

Absorption: mass passes from the satellite to the dominant, to the degree KABS · sat · max(0, S − SMIN) (KABS = 1.0, SMIN = 0.05). The lower bound SMIN prevents disappearance — the satellite shrinks without extinguishing — and makes the absorption self-limiting (it dies out at the bound). A spiral does not occur: the dominant grows (up to ~0.69 under full satellisation) but remains limited by its own fatigue. Orbit: the display position of the satellite glides continuously toward the dominant, by min(0.42; KORB · sat) (KORB = 0.6) — the more satellised, the closer, up to entry, without overlap.

*** (5) Ruler debouncing (resolution of a limit cycle)

Under strong encapsulation (low viability optimum or strong dominance), the ruler of an encapsulated structure flickered frame by frame between self-rule and encapsulation — and thereby drove a genuine limit cycle: the size oscillated by up to ±0.05, the relational structures jumped visibly. A temporal debouncing remedies this: a ruler change must persist for RULER_DWELL = 0.6 s to take hold. This resolves the limit cycle (amplitude from 53·10⁻³ to ~1·10⁻³) without suppressing the genuine encapsulation — it takes place, only stably. The oscillation was thus a flicker-driven limit cycle, not a deep instability; stabilising the ruler resolves it. Such limit cycles and their damping by hysteresis or dwell time belong to the standard repertoire of nonlinear dynamics (Strogatz 1994); the debouncing acts as a hysteretic regulator (Ashby 1956).

*Validation.* Verified: the perfect state remains untouched (sat = 0, no orbit, no oscillation, even under strong erosion and absorption); in no tested regime does a limit cycle occur; the absorption does not spiral (lower bound, dominant limited by fatigue); and the erosion is reversible (re-emancipation of the relation).

** Gini and reciprocity per structure; contributions to the global level

Beyond the global measures (Gini of the sizes, measured reciprocity), the digital twin resolves both onto the level of the individual structure — in two complementary readings: the structure's own (internal) situation and its contribution to the global level. All four measures were verified headless; at the perfect state the deficits and contributions vanish, the own reciprocity stands at ~1.

- *Own reciprocity R[x].* The relational health of the structure itself: R[x] = clamp[(0.5 + 0.5 · instance health[x]) − (0.40 · remainder[x] + 0.45 · satellisation[x] + 0.10 · sclerosis[x] + 0.10 · rupture[x])]. High when the structure is well mediated and neither satellised nor sclerosed; low for a satellite as for a sclerosed dominant. Headless: at the perfect state R = 1 for all; under dominance the dominant keeps a high own reciprocity (~0.9), while the satellites fall (the most strongly satellised the deepest).
- *Internal Gini G[x].* The inequality within the structure — the Gini of its sequences — as the "within" counterpart to the global "between" Gini. Low for balanced sequences, high for a concentrated or skewed structure.
- *Contribution to global inequality.* Signed: sign(S[x] − 1/N) · Σⱼ|S[x] − S[j]|/(2N). Positive (over-dimensioned) = the structure enlarges the inequality; negative (under-endowed) = it suffers it. The sum of the magnitudes yields exactly the global Gini; zero at the equality state. In the interface: amber if the structure aggravates the inequality, blue if it suffers it.
- *Contribution to the weakening of global reciprocity.* A per-structure decomposition of the deficit (1 − R): each term of the global formula is attributed to the structure (health deficit, share of inequality, rupture, sclerosis, unresolved remainder). The sum yields the global deficit, zero at the perfect state. Headless (dominance): the dominant weakens global reciprocity the most — although its own reciprocity is the highest. Precisely this contrast makes the two readings legible: the same structure can function well for itself and at the same time undermine systemic reciprocity the most.

Display: the four measures appear per structure (columns A/B/C/D) in the right-hand block of the central panel, in the same column logic as the instance forces.

** The cascade of the three inequality levels

The Theory of the Relation thinks inequality on three nested levels. Up to v58 the model grasped them only as the global Gini of the sizes and, per structure, as the internal Gini of the sequences. Version 59 makes the full resolution explicit and connects the three levels as a cascade — from the widest (between the structures) to the narrowest (between the actor categories within a sequence). It is a pure measurement layer: it is read directly from the existing state (S, g, act) and adds no new force — the mechanics of the model remain unchanged.

- *Transversal level (between the structures).* The inequality of the relational sizes S between the four structures — the global Gini already shown above. It is the widest level and coincides with the Gini of the sizes; at the symmetric state (equally large structures) it is zero.
- *Horizontal level (between the sequences of a structure).* The mean of the internal Ginis G[x] over the four structures: how unequally the sequences within a structure are dimensioned. At the symmetric state (equally large sequences) likewise zero.
- *Vertical level (between the actor categories of a sequence).* New in v59. The status gap between the four actor categories within a sequence. The categories are ranked by status (Established = 3 > Aspirants = 2 > Conservatives = 1 > Disappointed = 0); for each sequence the model computes the Gini of this status distribution (mean absolute difference / 2 · mean), weighted by the size of the sequence, and averages over the sequences — globally as well as per structure (A/B/C/D). A sequence with only one category has vertical inequality zero; a sequence in which the extremes coexist (Established beside Disappointed) reaches a high value.

*A positive baseline of the vertical level.* Unlike the transversal and the horizontal, the vertical level does not vanish at the symmetric state. Since each sequence carries from the outset a mixture of the four categories (default composition 0.22 / 0.28 / 0.28 / 0.22 for Established / Aspirants / Conservatives / Disappointed), it retains a positive baseline of ~0.40. This is theoretically coherent: status inequality is proper to any sequence that contains a spectrum of actor categories — it does not presuppose structural domination. Domination modulates this baseline, it does not produce it.

*The cascade and its coupling.* The three levels form a nested hierarchy of scales — society → structure → sequence → actor — which the digital twin reads simultaneously. Theoretically central is their coupling: under domination the levels do not move in the same direction. The dominant structure, which takes in and socialises ever more actors, homogenises its apex — its inner differences flatten out (low horizontal and vertical inequality) — while the satellised structures stratify: their vertical inequality rises, since declassing and marginalisation drive their actors apart. Thus the transversal inequality between the structures translates downward into the vertical stratification of the dominated. This continues, at the level of the actor categories, what already holds for the sequences: satellisation reads on every scale. With this, the stratification set out in document 1 (Mobility and stratification) — the vertical, horizontal, and cross-structural mobility — receives, for each of its axes, its own inequality measure.

*Display.* In the central panel a new block "Cascade · 3 levels (TR)" shows the three levels as bars (transversal, horizontal, vertical) and below them a row of the vertical inequality per structure (A/B/C/D), with the legend "the apex homogenises, the dominated stratify". The block sits between the evolution curve and the instance forces.

*State of the outputs.* The cascade is at present a display of the interface; it is continuously computed from the living state but is not yet part of the deterministic result outputs (CSV time series and SVG figures, 5.16 and v48), which still carry the global Gini and the internal Gini per structure. Its inclusion in the outputs — in particular a column per level and a figure of the three levels over time — is an obvious next step.

* User interface and controls

The scene shows the four relational structures as glowing circles in a diamond arrangement. Top left lies — as a horizontal panel of four blocks — the central evaluation: (1) the evolution curve of Gini and reciprocity together with the global bars (Gini, reciprocity, overload); (2) the cascade of the three inequality levels (transversal, horizontal, vertical) with the vertical inequality per structure; (3) the instance forces per structure (relation/sequence/sub-sequence/control, columns A/B/C/D); (4) the four per-structure measures (own reciprocity, internal Gini, contribution to inequality, weakening of reciprocity). The right-hand control panel contains the legend, the actor-composition bars, the sequence inspector, the chain editor, the structure-specific demographics (A/B/C/D selection, composition and weight controls), the CSV import, and the timeline.

*Visual coding of the structures.* Each structure is an inner circle in its own colour; its radius encodes the size (r ∝ √S). If a second, somewhat larger ring appears around it — the "double-circle" sign — then the structure is no longer autonomous: its regime (ruler) is that of another structure. Decisive is the colour of this ring: it carries the colour of the dominating structure. The inner circle thus says "who I am and how big", the outer ring "under whose regime I fall" — the visual counterpart to the regime note in the bars (·autonomous vs. ·▸X) and the visible expression of satellisation: a satellite orbits the dominant and follows its regime, and the ring shows which one. Two further, transient rings are to be distinguished from this: a dashed orange ring during an ongoing rupture and a pulsing white ring on the currently stimulated structure. Golden, moving dots show migrating established actors; the smaller dots inside are the sequences, their lines the chain.

| Demographic anchoring | how closely the sizes follow the population data |

*Defaults of the perfect state.* At start-up all controls stand on a non-deviant reference value and the demographics is symmetric (equally large relational structures) — inequality 0, reciprocity ~1. Mobility is here explicitly active: migration and a medium declassing (0.5) mean that newcomers do arrive declassed but resocialise in a healthy relation; and a medium value for "advantage for dynamic structures" (0.5) gives circulation its weight against the mere population count, in accordance with the primacy of circulation. Since the start state is symmetric, these values remain without effect there; they unfold only on asymmetric data. The control "weight of number" is not a pure display option but a determinant: it determines how strongly the relational size follows the demographic one — which is why it actually changes inequality and reciprocity (the demographic and the relational determination carry different inequalities, which forms precisely the core of the theory).

*Reproducibility:* a random seed fixes the small initial perturbation; the same seed and the same settings yield the same trajectory. This makes the digital twin a comparative analysis tool.

** Multilingualism of the interface and the outputs (DE/EN/FR)

*Trilingualism (v52–v58).* The digital twin is a single application that switches between German, English, and French (the DE·EN·FR pill at the top right of the central panel; English default). A central dictionary holds every string in three languages; the language switch instantly rewrites the entire interface — labels, tooltips, structure names on the circles, actor categories, the regime note (autonom / autonomous / autonome), and the control labels. The pedagogical tooltips were revised for first-time users (v55).

*Localisation of the outputs.* The exported files too follow the language: file names (Zeitreihen.csv / time_series.csv / series_temporelles.csv; transitions.csv; the six figures), titles and legends of the SVG figures, and the status messages. Deliberate exception: the CSV column headers always stay English and machine-readable (year, size_A, reciprocity, struct_from …), so that analysis scripts work language-independently; the actor categories as data values, by contrast, follow the language (Established / Aspirants / Conservatives / Disappointed). The CSV files are prefixed with a UTF-8 BOM, so that umlauts appear correctly in Excel. Verified headless in all three languages.
