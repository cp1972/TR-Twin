# Empirical control parc — provenance and reading

Derived from `instances_extract_contract.csv` (the 60 instances of the art-and-media
subcorpus) by labelling the **control** instances, their **target sequence**, and an
indicative **weight**. A row is a control instance when it evaluates, sorts, certifies,
consecrates or regulates (rather than produces or distributes).

**Corpus note.** This is the art-and-media subset, hence A- and D-heavy. B and C are sparse:
B has *no* real control instance here and is represented by two **illustrative** entries for
contrast; C has one real instance (actuarial). Weights are indicative, spans run from each
instance's founding (catalogue) to 1966 at a steady authority level.

## A — Culture (the developed control apparatus)

- **A·seqA — certification / examination.** Universities (Cambridge, St Andrews, London,
  Harvard — w 0.60), colleges (University College, Reading, City of London, Anderson's, Bible
  Training — 0.45), grammar/secondary schools (Aberdeen Grammar, Shrewsbury — 0.35). They
  examine and certify the core cultural producers (the grading example).
- **A·seqD — criticism / consecration / moralisation.** Secular Review (0.42), literary
  societies (Elizabethan, Manchester Literary Club — 0.40), V&A museum (0.45, consecration of
  taste), secular societies (National, Leicester — 0.35, orthodoxy), Sunday-school organs
  (Oddfellows, Sunday School Times — 0.30, moralisation). They sort works and police cultural
  orthodoxy.

## C — Economy

- **C·seqD — temperance_and_general_provi (0.35).** Actuarial science as an instrument of
  economic regulation.

## D — Media (emerging)

- **D·seqC — Financial Times (0.45).** A daily doing economic evaluation.
- **D·seqD — Reuters (0.60).** Gatekeeping agency; the data supplier of journalism to this day.

## B — Politics (illustrative, no real control instance in this corpus)

- B·seqB parliamentary_oversight (0.45), B·seqD royal_commission (0.40). Kept for contrast.

## Excluded as reporting (not control)

building_news (D·seqA), railway_review (D·seqA), municipal_journal (D·seqB), and the
newspapers, journals and presses of production/distribution.

## Empirical finding

Contrary to the theoretical prior A < B < C < D, the **derived** capacity at 1950 is

: A 1.55  >  D 1.29  >  B 1.25 (illustrative)  >  C 1.12

This is expected, not a bug. The corpus captures the **birth and expansion of the media
sphere** as it frees itself from State tutelage to become an independent relational structure —
a process the 20th century accentuates (journalism, publishing leaving A for C, computing, the
Internet). Culture (A) carries the long-established control apparatus (certification *and*
criticism); media (D) is still building its own, with **Reuters** the durable anchor. The
gradation is read off the parc, not decreed.

## Authority curves (refinement)

Weights are no longer flat: each control instance carries an **authority curve**
(founding → peak → decline/sustain) with closures where they apply. The structuring instances are
sourced:

- **Reuters** (D·seqD): 1851 founding → 1858 the *Times* and the British press subscribe → national
  and imperial news institution, sustained high through the 20th c. (Britannica; *The Power of
  News*, OUP).
- **Financial Times** (D·seqC): 1888 founding (London Financial Guide → Financial Times), the
  "stockbroker's bible"; 1945 merger with the Financial News makes it the leading financial
  authority (Britannica; Wikipedia; Historic Newspapers).
- **University of London** (A·seqA): 1836 examining/degree-awarding board; **1858 external degrees**
  opened to candidates outside any college — the certification ladder for working-class autodidacts;
  1900 teaching university (Britannica; University of London, "Our history").
- **Victoria & Albert Museum** (A·seqD): 1852 founding (Museum of Manufactures, after the 1851
  Exhibition); renamed V&A 1899; new building 1909 — consecration of design and taste.
- **Secular Review** (A·seqD): 1876 → peak ~1882 → closes ~1888 (becomes *The Agnostic Journal*).

The remaining control instances carry plausible curves anchored on their real founding (e.g.
University College 1826, Anderson's 1796, National Secular Society 1866, Manchester Literary Club
1862, Temperance & General Provident Institution 1840). Consequence: control capacity is now
**time-varying** — Culture's apparatus is high early (the universities pre-exist), Media's builds up
across the 19th–20th c. (Reuters, then the FT), which is exactly what the deepening figure reads.
