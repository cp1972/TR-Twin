# Historical grounding of `instances_probe_demo.csv`

The `poids` (weight, 0–1) curves are **illustrative empirical trajectories** for six mediation
instances, anchored on documented milestones (founding, peak, decline, closure). They are
teaching curves, not measured circulation series — magnitudes are indicative; the **inflection
years** are historical. Sources are named per instance.

The weight is read as a *target share* for the instance's cell `g[structure][sequence]` (a soft
anchor, not a clamp), aggregated by **maximum** when several instances share a cell.

---

## Culture core — A·seqA

**Mechanics' Magazine** (1823–1872) — `A·seqA`
- 1823 founded in London by Joseph Clinton Robertson, a political radical of the early working-class
  press; tied to the Mechanics' Institute movement.
- 1820s–1840s peak: weekly diffusion of industrial knowledge to working mechanics; coverage of the
  Rainhill Trials (1829) and the ferment of the Industrial Revolution.
- decline after Robertson's death (1852); the title runs on under successors and ends c. 1872.
- *Sources:* Wikipedia (Joseph Clinton Robertson; Mechanics' Magazine); New York Public Library blog;
  Lehigh Library exhibits.

**The Secular Review** (1876–1888) — `A·seqA`
- 1876 founded as a freethought weekly within the secularist/agnostic ferment of the period.
- modest peak in the 1880s; transformed into *The Agnostic Journal* c. 1889.
- *Source:* standard freethought-press histories (approximate magnitudes).

## Publishing bridge — A·seqD (the D-sequence of Culture, g[A][D])

**Bakunin Press** (1907–1948) — `A·seqD`
- 1907 established in London by Guy A. Aldred (anarchist/freethought printing).
- modest interwar activity; lineage continues into the Strickland Press (from 1939); ends c. 1948.
- *Source:* Modern Records Centre, University of Warwick (Strickland Press / Bakunin Press / Guy A.
  Aldred, 1907–1948).

## Media — D·seqD

**Cotton Factory Times** (1885–1937) — `D·seqD`
- 1885 (16 Jan) founded by John Andrew (Ashton Evening Reporter) for Lancashire/Cheshire cotton
  operatives; backed by the spinners' and weavers' unions though privately owned.
- peak in the Edwardian years — reportedly ~53,000 copies an issue by 1900.
- circulation falls after 1910 (~20,000), halves again by 1918, declines with the cotton industry.
- 1937 (31 Dec) ceased, sales ~2,500.
- *Sources:* Wikipedia; British Newspaper Archive blog; Lancashire Cotton Cartoons.

**Reuters** (1851– …) — `D·seqD`
- 1851 founded by Paul Julius Reuter at the London Royal Exchange (commercial telegrams).
- 1858 the *Times* and other English papers subscribe — Reuters becomes indispensable to the British
  press; reputation built on exclusive reporting.
- late 19th–20th c.: "the news agency of the British Empire", a national and imperial institution;
  influence sustained and, by some accounts, greatest at the end of the 20th century.
- (curve capped at 1990 for the demo timeline.)
- *Sources:* Britannica; Encyclopedia.com; *The Power of News: The History of Reuters* (OUP).

**Daily Herald** (1912–1964) — `D·seqD`
- 1912 (15 Apr) relaunched as a labour-movement daily (from the 1911 print-strike bulletin); Dyson's
  cartoons drive early sales (~230,000), then financial struggle.
- weekly and anti-war during WWI; resumes daily 1919; handed to the TUC/Labour 1922.
- 1929–30 Odhams Press takes a 51% stake; promotion drives.
- **June 1933**: first daily worldwide to 2 million net sales — world's best-selling daily for ~3 years.
- decline thereafter (eroded by the Daily Mirror); 1964 ceased, relaunched as *The Sun*.
- *Sources:* Wikipedia; National Science and Media Museum; openDemocracy; British Newspaper Archive.
